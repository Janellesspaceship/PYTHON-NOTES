**1.What problem are you solving?**

`Tax Map Kenya 2024`

The Kenya Finance Bill 2024 introduced many tax changes aimed at raising about KSh 302 billion for the 2024/25 budget. However, the bill was very long and complicated, and many of the proposals felt like they would increase the cost of living for ordinary citizens.


Most people could not clearly understand how these tax changes would affect them in real life, especially for basic goods and services like bread, sanitary products, and digital work.


My project solves this problem by breaking down the Finance Bill into simple, visual data stories. It helps people clearly see who is affected by each tax change and how much they would likely pay.


**2.What tools did you use?**


I used Python and data analysis tools to turn the Finance Bill into understandable insights:


`Python (Pandas & PDFPlumber)`: To extract and clean data from the Finance Bill PDF and related government reports.


`Matplotlib & Seaborn`: To create charts comparing current taxes with proposed changes.


`Plotly`: To build an interactive tool where users can estimate how the new taxes would affect them personally.


`Markdown`: To document my process and explain the findings clearly.


**3.What insights did you discover?**


`Progressivity Gap`: Some taxes, like the motor vehicle tax, were actually unfair. Wealthier car owners ended up paying a smaller percentage of their car’s value compared to owners of cheaper cars.


`Eco Paradox`: Some “environmental” taxes, like the Eco Levy, were applied to essential items such as diapers and electronics, which could hurt low-income families and small businesses.


`Privacy Trade-off`: The bill proposed giving the tax authority access to personal data without court approval, raising concerns about privacy rights.


`Public Sentiment`: Most public complaints focused on a few key issues like bread, motor vehicles, and financial services. These were the same areas that led lawmakers to remove or revise several proposals.


**4.How would businesses or communities benefit from your work?**


`For communities`: It makes tax changes easy to understand, showing how policies like VAT on bread could affect everyday living costs and food security.


`For businesses`: It helps freelancers, startups, and digital workers understand how new taxes might increase their costs so they can plan better.


`For policy groups`: It provides clear, data-based evidence that can be used to advocate for fairer and more balanced tax policies in government discussions.
